I Tested 15 "Free" Software Tools. Only 4 Were Actually Free. Here's What the Other 11 Hid From You.

By BestTechToolsHub Editorial Team · 7 min read · We installed and used every tool for 7+ days

"Free" is the most powerful word in software marketing. But in our testing of 15 popular "free" tools, only 4 delivered genuinely free, usable functionality without strings. The other 11 used dark patterns, hidden limitations, or outright deception. Here's what we found — and which free tools are actually worth your time.

The 4 Genuinely Free Tools (No Catches)

1. 7-Zip: File compression. Open source, no ads, no limitations, handles every format. Replaces WinZip ($39.99/year) completely. Verdict: 100% free, no catch.

2. LibreOffice: Full office suite (Word, Excel, PowerPoint equivalents). Open source, opens all Microsoft formats, no subscription. For 95% of users, it replaces Microsoft 365 ($99.99/year). Verdict: 100% free, no catch.

3. HandBrake: Video converter. Open source, converts to any format, no watermarks, no time limits. Replaces UniConverter ($59.99/year) for most users. Verdict: 100% free, no catch.

4. DaVinci Resolve: Professional video editing. The free version is Hollywood-grade (used in actual movies). No watermark, no time limit, no feature lock beyond the most advanced color tools. Verdict: 100% free for 99% of users.

The 11 "Free" Tools That Weren't Actually Free

5–8. "Freemium" PDF editors (4 tools tested): All let you "edit for free" — then slap a giant watermark on your document when you try to save. Or limit you to 3 pages. Or require sign-up and then spam you. The truth: for actual PDF editing, use PDF24 Creator (free) or pay for PDFelement once.

9–10. "Free" antivirus (2 tools): One bundled a browser hijacker. The other showed full-screen pop-up ads every 20 minutes. Both had "upgrade now" buttons in every menu. Use Windows Defender (built-in, genuinely free) or the free version of Avast/AVG — they're honest about limitations.

11–12. "Free" video editors (2 tools): Both watermarked exports. One required creating an account and agreeing to let them use your videos for training. Use DaVinci Resolve or CapCut (free, no watermark).

13. "Free" data recovery: Free scan (useless — you can't recover anything without paying). Classic bait-and-switch. Use Recuva (free for basic recovery) or the free scan of paid tools to check if recovery is possible before paying.

14. "Free" VPN: Logs all your traffic and sells it (that's how they make money). Slow speeds. Data caps. Use ProtonVPN Free (genuinely free, no logs, no data cap) or pay for a reputable one.

15. "Free" photo editor: "Free" for 7 days, then $8.99/month. No mention of the trial on the download page. Use GIMP (free, open-source, Photoshop-level) or Photopea (free in-browser, looks exactly like Photoshop).

How to Spot a "Fake Free" Tool in 10 Seconds

  • If it requires your credit card for "free" — it's not free.
  • If the word "free" is followed by an asterisk (*) — read the fine print.
  • If "free" means "free trial" — it's not free.
  • If it's open source (code publicly available) — it's almost always genuinely free.
  • If you can't find pricing on the website — they're hiding it. Run.
Golden rule: For any category of software, check if an open-source alternative exists first. Open-source tools are community-audited, can't hide malware, and never have hidden paywalls. We list the best free/open-source options in every review on this site — check our reviews page.
